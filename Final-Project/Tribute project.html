A Tribute to Jomo Kenyatta

Jomo Kenyatta was a Kenyan statesman, political leader and one of the principal figures in Kenya's struggle for independence. He became Kenya's first Prime Minister in 1963 and served as the country's first President from 1964 until his death in 1978.


His life remains an important part of Kenya's history and national identity. He is remembered for his role in the transition from colonial rule to an independent Kenyan state.

Jomo Kenyatta Timeline

20 October 1893

Jomo Kenyatta was born in Gatundu, in what is now Kiambu County, Kenya.

1920s

Kenyatta became increasingly involved in political activism and the struggle for African rights in colonial Kenya.

1928

He became the first Secretary General of the Kikuyu Central Association.

1938

His influential book Facing Mount Kenya was published.

1952

Kenyatta was arrested by the colonial government during the period of the Mau Mau uprising.

1961

After years of detention, Kenyatta was released, becoming an increasingly central figure in Kenya's independence movement.

12 December 1963

Kenya gained independence from British colonial rule. Jomo Kenyatta became the country's first Prime Minister.

12 December 1964

Kenya became a republic, and Jomo Kenyatta became the country's first President.

1964–1978

Kenyatta served as President of Kenya during the country's early years as an independent republic.

22 August 1978

Jomo Kenyatta died in Mombasa at the age of 84.

His Legacy

Independence

Played a major role in Kenya's transition from colonial rule to independence.

Leadership

Served as Kenya's first Prime Minister and first President.

Education

His writings, including Facing Mount Kenya, contributed to international understanding of Kenyan society and culture.

National Memory

His name continues to be associated with major Kenyan institutions, roads, airports and monuments.
